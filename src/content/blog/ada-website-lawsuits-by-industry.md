**ADA website lawsuits** do not land evenly. A small number of industries draw almost all of them, and the split has barely moved in years. This page pulls the industry numbers from the two most recent UsableNet lawsuit reports, explains why those sectors are targeted, and lists what to check first in each one. For the overall 2026 filing numbers, the states with the most suits and four recent cases, read our guide to [2026 ADA lawsuits and recent website cases](/blog/ada-lawsuits-2026).

## ADA Website Lawsuits by Industry: The 2025 and 2026 Numbers

UsableNet, an accessibility company, reviews digital accessibility lawsuits by hand and sorts them by the defendant's industry. Its 2025 count covers federal courts plus New York and California state courts from January 1 to December 15, 2025: 3,195 federal cases and 1,919 state cases. Its 2026 midyear report covers filings through June 14, 2026, in federal courts and key state courts in California, New York, Florida and Illinois.

| Industry | Share of 2025 lawsuits | Share of first-half 2026 lawsuits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E-commerce | 70% | 79% |
| Food service | 21% | 14% |
| Healthcare | 2% | 1% |
| Fitness and wellness | 2% | under 1% |
| Entertainment and leisure | 1% | under 1% |
| Travel and hospitality | 1% | under 1% |
| Education | 1% | under 1% |
| Banking and financial | under 1% | under 1% |
| Real estate agencies and properties | under 1% | under 1% |
| Insurance, automotive, telecom, digital media | under 1% each | under 1% each |

Sources: [UsableNet 2025 Year-End Digital Accessibility Lawsuit Report](https://info.usablenet.com/hubfs/Remediated%20-%202025_Year-End_Digital_Accessibility_Lawsuit_Report_FINAL.pdf) and [UsableNet 2026 Midyear Digital Accessibility Lawsuit Report](https://info.usablenet.com/hubfs/REMEDIATED%202026-Midyear-Digital-Accessibility-Lawsuit-Report.pdf). Each report also lists an "Other" row of about 1%.

These are shares of lawsuits UsableNet tracked, not of every demand letter or every state court. Seyfarth Shaw, which counts only federal filings, found 3,117 federal website lawsuits in 2025 ([Seyfarth Shaw](https://www.adatitleiii.com/2026/03/federal-court-website-accessibility-lawsuit-filings-bounce-back-in-2025/)). The two series are built differently and should not be added together.

## Why These Industries Get Sued for Website Accessibility

The ADA's Title III covers "public accommodations," and the statute lists 12 kinds in [42 U.S.C. § 12181(7)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/12181). They include a "clothing store, hardware store, shopping center, or other sales or rental establishment," a "restaurant, bar, or other establishment serving food or drink," and the "professional office of a health care provider." UsableNet's reports point to the same reasons the top sectors keep coming back:

- **They match the ADA's original categories.** Stores, restaurants, doctors and gyms are named in the statute, so a plaintiff can argue the website is how the public reaches those goods and services.
- **Every step can be tested.** A store has search, product pages, a cart and checkout. A restaurant has a menu, ordering and reservations. Each step is a place where an unlabeled button or a keyboard trap can stop a purchase.
- **A link to a physical place.** In some courts a plaintiff has to show a connection between the website and a physical location. "Find a store," "order for pickup" and "book a table" make that connection easy.
- **Popular brands are easy to name.** UsableNet says plaintiffs find it easier to allege they meant to shop at, and return to, a well-known site.

## Small Businesses and Big Brands Are Both Targets

Most defendants are not household names. In 2025, 64% of the companies sued had under $25 million in annual revenue, according to UsableNet. In the first half of 2026, UsableNet moved to a $50 million cutoff: 68% of suits were against companies below it, 22% against companies between $50 million and $1 billion, and 10% against companies above $1 billion.

Big retailers are sued too. UsableNet found that 36% of the top 500 e-commerce retailers received at least one accessibility lawsuit in 2025, and 20% had been sued in 2026 by mid-year.

## Getting Sued Once Raises the Odds of a Second Suit

UsableNet counted 1,427 lawsuits in 2025 against companies that had already been sued, 45% of all federal cases. Its report says plaintiff firms watch past defendants, and a single lawsuit rarely ends the exposure unless the site is actually fixed. A settlement that pays money without fixing the website leaves the same barriers for the next plaintiff, which is the problem the Department of Justice raised in the Fashion Nova case covered in our [2026 ADA lawsuits guide](/blog/ada-lawsuits-2026).

Widgets did not change this. UsableNet counted 1,416 lawsuits in 2025 against companies that already had an accessibility widget on the site, between 95 and 155 every month. A widget does not fix the code a screen reader reads.

## Demand Letters Do Not Show Up in These Numbers

Many businesses hear from a plaintiff's lawyer by letter before, or instead of, a lawsuit. Those letters are not court filings, so no industry count includes them.
