Siteimprove shows up in almost every enterprise accessibility shortlist, and for large organizations managing thousands of pages across many teams, it can be the right tool. But if you are here searching for a **Siteimprove alternative**, the reason is usually one of two things: the price, or the process to get a price at all.

## What Siteimprove Actually Costs

Siteimprove does not publish pricing anywhere. Every review site, G2, Capterra and SaaSworthy included, lists it as "contact vendor." Based on independent reporting and buyer-reported figures, small accessibility-only engagements run around £8,800 (roughly $11,000) a year for about 1,000 pages, mid-market bundles that combine accessibility with content quality or SEO modules land in the $8,000 to $20,000 range, and large enterprise contracts covering the full platform commonly reach $30,000 to $80,000 or more per year ([independent pricing analysis](https://ratedwithai.com/blog/siteimprove-pricing-2026)).

Pricing is modular and scales with page count, which modules you license, and contract length, and it is negotiated through a sales process rather than available to sign up for directly.

## Where Siteimprove Fits, and Where It Does Not

Siteimprove is a genuine digital governance suite: accessibility monitoring alongside SEO auditing, content quality checks, analytics and policy management, aimed at organizations that want one platform across marketing, content and compliance teams. If that describes your organization and budget, it is a reasonable choice.

If you specifically need **accessibility scanning and monitoring** — a real audit engine, clear fixes mapped to WCAG criteria, and scheduled rescans that catch regressions — and you do not need the rest of the suite, you are likely paying for modules you will not use, on a timeline that starts with a sales call rather than a scan.
