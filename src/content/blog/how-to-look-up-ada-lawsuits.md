You can **look up ADA lawsuits** against any business, or by any plaintiff, in public federal court records. If you were just served, this is Step 6 of our [first-week guide to what to do after an ADA lawsuit](/blog/ada-lawsuit-what-to-do): find the plaintiff's other cases and hand them to your lawyer. This page shows where to search, what it costs, which filter finds ADA cases, and what to write down.

*This is general information, not legal advice. Share what you find with a lawyer who defends ADA cases.*

## Why Look Up ADA Lawsuits at All?

There are three common reasons, and each changes what you search for:

- **You were sued and want to know who is suing you.** Search the plaintiff and the plaintiff's law firm. Courts have started to look harder at serial filings: in February 2026, Judge Jeannette Vargas in the Southern District of New York ordered discovery on whether the plaintiff in *Fernandez v. Cuddle Clones* had really tried to buy from the site, after noting more than 57 similar suits ([Seyfarth Shaw](https://www.adatitleiii.com/2026/02/ny-judge-questions-plaintiffs-pet-plushie-purchase-tale-orders-jurisdictional-discovery-in-website-access-suit/)).
- **You want to know if your own business has been sued before.** Repeat suits are common, and a past settlement may include a fix deadline you still owe.
- **You are checking a company you plan to buy, partner with or work for.** Past ADA cases show whether its website has been a target.

## Where To Look Up ADA Lawsuits: Sources Compared

| Source | Cost | What it covers | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [CourtListener RECAP Archive](https://www.courtlistener.com/?type=r) | Free | Federal dockets and filings that PACER users have shared through RECAP | A first, free search by business or plaintiff name |
| [PACER Case Locator](https://pacer.uscourts.gov/) | $0.10 per page, $3 cap per document, not billed under $30 a quarter | A nationwide index of federal court cases | A complete federal search when RECAP has gaps |
| The court named on your summons | Free at the courthouse; PACER fees online | That court's full docket | Reading every filing in your own case |
| State court records systems | Varies by state | State court cases, which PACER does not include | Cases filed under state law in state court |

## How To Look Up ADA Lawsuits Step by Step

### Step 1: Start With the Summons

If you were served, the summons already gives you the court, the case number and the plaintiff's lawyer. PACER's own advice is to search by case number rather than by party name, because a case number takes you straight to the case, while a common party name can return many billable pages.

### Step 2: Search the RECAP Archive for Free

Go to the [RECAP Archive search on CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/?type=r) and search the plaintiff's name in quotes. Then repeat with your business name and with the plaintiff's law firm. Anything another PACER user has already bought is free to read here. Coverage is not complete, so a search with no results does not mean there are no cases.

### Step 3: Search the PACER Case Locator

Register for a [PACER account](https://pacer.uscourts.gov/), open the PACER Case Locator and run a civil search by party name. To keep the results to ADA cases, filter the nature of suit to **446, Americans with Disabilities - Other**. Narrow the date range and the state when you can, because PACER charges for search result pages, including empty ones.

### Step 4: Search the Plaintiff's Law Firm

The same law firm may file website cases for several plaintiffs. Searching the attorney or firm name can show a pattern the plaintiff's name alone misses: the same complaint text, the same barriers and many defendants in a short period.

### Step 5: Check State Court if Your Case Is There

PACER covers federal courts only. If you were sued in state court, for example under California's Unruh Act, search that state's court records system. See [California website accessibility law](/state-accessibility-laws/california) and [New York website accessibility law](/state-accessibility-laws/new-york) for the two states with the most state-law activity.

## What To Record for Each ADA Lawsuit You Find

Put each case in one row of a spreadsheet. This is the list your lawyer will ask for:

| Field | Why it matters |
| --- | --- |
| Case name and number | Lets your lawyer pull the docket without searching again |
| Court and date filed | Shows how many suits were filed, where and how fast |
| Plaintiff's law firm | Shows whether the same firm files for several plaintiffs |
| Barriers named in the complaint | Shows whether the complaint is copied from case to case |
| Status or outcome | Dismissed, settled or still open, and how quickly |

If your search shows the same plaintiff and firm filing near-identical complaints, your lawyer may use that when deciding whether to challenge standing, settle early or fix the site and argue the claim is moot. Our guide to [ADA lawsuit mootness](/blog/ada-lawsuit-mootness) explains that last option, and the [ADA lawsuit process](/blog/ada-lawsuit-process) explains where each choice fits.

## What a Lawsuit Search Cannot Tell You

- **It will not show demand letters.** Letters sent before a case is filed are private. If that is what you got, read [what an ADA demand letter means and how to respond](/blog/ada-demand-letter).
- **It will not show settlement terms.** Private settlements are not usually filed, so the docket may show only a dismissal.
- **It will not tell you whether your site has the same barriers.** Only testing your site can do that. To see which sectors are sued most, read [which industries get sued for ADA website lawsuits](/blog/ada-website-lawsuits-by-industry).
